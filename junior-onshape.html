Onshape guide

Sketch, build parts, make assemblies and produce working drawings.

What is Onshape?

Onshape is a professional CAD (computer aided design) program that runs in your browser. Your work is saved automatically to your account, and you can open it on any computer.

The parts of a document

  • Part Studio: where you sketch and build individual parts.
  • Assembly: where you put parts together and make them move.
  • Drawing: where you make a dimensioned working drawing from a part or assembly.

Good habits

  • Give each document, part and feature a clear name.
  • Work in millimetres and keep your units consistent.
  • Save versions at important points so you can go back.
  • Model one part at a time, then assemble.
This page is a guide. Your teacher's brief, instructions and deadlines always come first, and menus can look slightly different from the screenshots and steps your class uses.

Sketching

  1. Choose a plane (Top, Front or Right) and start a Sketch.
  2. Draw with the line, rectangle or circle tools.
  3. Add dimensions with the dimension tool so the sketch has exact sizes.
  4. Add constraints (for example, equal, parallel, perpendicular, concentric) so the shape keeps its rules.
  5. A fully defined sketch is one that cannot move. Onshape shows under-defined parts in a different colour from fully defined ones, so keep adding dimensions and constraints until it is fully defined.
  6. Finish the sketch (the green tick).
Tip: draw the shape roughly first, then dimension it. Sketch on the plane that gives you the view you want.

Features

A feature turns a sketch into a 3D shape.

FeatureWhat it does
ExtrudePulls a sketch out to make a 3D solid (add) or cuts into a part (remove)
FilletRounds an edge
ChamferCuts a slanted edge
HoleMakes a drilled hole of a set size
ShellHollows a solid, leaving walls of a set thickness
Mirror, PatternCopies a feature (in a line, a circle or across a plane)
RevolveSpins a sketch around an axis to make round parts

Example: a lamp base

  1. Sketch a circle on the Top plane and dimension its diameter.
  2. Extrude it to the base thickness.
  3. Fillet the top edge for a smooth finish.
  4. Add a hole for the cable or switch.

Assemblies

  1. Create an Assembly tab and insert your parts.
  2. The first part is usually fixed so it does not move.
  3. Add mates to position each part and decide how it can move.
  4. Drag the parts to check they move the way you expect.
MateHow the part moves
FastenedNot at all: the parts are fixed together
RevoluteRotates around one axis (a hinge or a shaft)
SliderSlides in one direction
CylindricalRotates and slides along the same axis
PlanarMoves on a flat surface

Exploded view

An exploded view separates the parts so you can see how they fit together. Create it from your assembly, move the parts apart along their assembly directions, and include it in your folio.

Drawings

  1. Create a Drawing from your part or assembly and choose a paper size and a scale.
  2. Add the standard views: front, plan (top) and side.
  3. Add an isometric view for a 3D picture.
  4. Add dimensions that show the overall size and important features.
  5. Fill in the title block (name, title, scale, date).
  6. Export as a PDF to put in your folio.

Drawing checklist

  • Are all the key dimensions shown, and only once each?
  • Is the scale stated?
  • Are the views lined up and labelled?
  • Is the title block complete?
