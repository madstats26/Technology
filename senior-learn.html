    Getting the marks

    • Count the points. A 6-mark question is often split, for example 2 + 4 or 3 + 3, or two answers worth 3 marks each. If it says "outline two", give two separate points.
    • Answer in the context. Use the product, system or situation in the question instead of a general definition.
    • Calculations: formula, substitution, answer, unit. Method earns marks even if the final number is wrong.
    • Sketches: use a ruler, make them large, and label every important part. An annotated sketch earns marks for the annotations.
    • Choose your 12. In Section A, quickly mark the three questions you are least sure of and leave them out.
    • Sections B and C: do (a) and (b) first, then choose (c) or (d) by which you can answer fully.
    • Do not leave blanks. Write what you know; partial answers earn marks.

    A worked example from the 2026 paper

    Q9 (6 marks). A static lever has a 250 N load 50 mm from the fulcrum and the effort 250 mm from the fulcrum. Calculate the effort and the mechanical advantage.

    Clockwise moment = anticlockwise moment, so effort × 250 = 250 × 50 = 12 500, and effort = 12 500 ÷ 250 = 50 N.

    Mechanical advantage = load ÷ effort = 250 ÷ 50 = 5.
