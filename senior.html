2026 paper at a glance
  • Section A: Dublin Airport solar farm, underwater treadmills, voltmeter reading, copper candelabra (properties and joining), shock-absorbing polymer foam, cybersecurity (2FA and strong passwords), fast fashion and planned obsolescence, graphic techniques (cordless drill), lever (effort and mechanical advantage), jigsaw motion and safety, fishbone diagram for soldering quality, smart materials, cam profile sketch, vacuum cleaner running cost and sensors, front elevation and plan of a climbing arch.

Project and portfolio

Project plan: 2027 Higher Level

Brief in short: in a context of your choice, with a focus on modern materials and processes, design and make a working model of a device, system or technical aid that supports independent living, wellbeing or social participation in an ageing society. Design folio 100 marks, artefact 100 marks.

Where to put your effort: folio sections 5 and 6 are worth 36 marks. On the artefact, production skills and functionality are worth 50 of 100. Environmental impact (10 marks) must show in both design and realisation. Headings and marks may vary by year, so check this plan against the official brief.

2027 project ideas: ageing society brief

From the class presentation on the 2027 Higher Level brief. Official completion date: Friday 19 March 2027. Every project needs a genuine electro-mechanical element, modern materials and processes, a maximum dimension of 500 mm, low voltage and visible operating features.

Rules from the 2027 coursework brief

  • Coursework must be available for assessment by Friday 19 March 2027. It must be your own individual work, completed in school under your class teacher's supervision, and must not leave the school.
  • Digital drawings or other manufacturing details must be included in hardcopy in the folio. If you use CAM, include the supporting CAD drawings to show the work is your own.
  • Important operating features must be clearly visible and easy to reach without dismantling. Use a low-voltage supply. Set up any specialised equipment yourself, with clear operating instructions.
  • A multimedia presentation can be at most 3 minutes, set up by you, and provided as a digital file ready to view on a school laptop or PC.
  • Reference every research source, including any material generated by AI software. Copying from the internet or other sources and presenting it as your own earns no marks. Suspected plagiarism, including AI use, will be investigated.

The four-question idea filter

QuestionWhat a good answer shows
User: who experiences the difficulty?A specific person or user group, and an understanding of their abilities.
Need: what task causes a real problem?One situation that can be observed, researched and improved.
System: what moves and how is it controlled?An actuator, a mechanism, accessible controls and a safety response.
Proof: how will success be measured?Loads, times, distances, accuracy or repeated operating cycles.

If one answer is still vague, the direction needs more research. Artefact making skill, working reliably and finish carry 65 of the 100 artefact marks, so a complex idea that barely works loses heavily.

More viable ideas

  • Smart medication dispenser: stepper-indexed tray, servo gate, timing and dose confirmation. Model only, never test with real medicine.
  • Motorised parcel retrieval unit: secure drawer or lift raises deliveries to an accessible height.
  • Adjustable reading or craft station: powered height and angle adjustment with large controls.
  • Automated bedside organiser: carousel presents glasses, phone or water without overreaching.
  • Accessible recycling station: motorised bin selector with automatic lid and fill sensor.
  • Mobility-aid storage lift: powered mechanism loads a small bag onto a walker or wheelchair.

Higher risk, higher reward

  • Self-levelling walker or wheelchair tray: two-axis servo gimbal with an accelerometer. Risk: tuning and backlash.
  • Chair-rise aid model: lead screw, scissor or four-bar mechanism. Risk: load, stability and safe limits.
  • Walker safety and braking system: obstacle detection activates a model brake. Risk: reliable detection and responsible safety claims.
  • Powered reaching and gripping aid: geared claw with grip sensing. Risk: weight, cable routing, grip force control.

Social participation and wellbeing

  • Interactive tabletop game system: motorised card or tile dispenser, accessible controls, automatic scoring.
  • Accessible gardening station: powered planter or tool platform, moisture sensing for irrigation.
  • Motorised hobby organiser: carousel presents paints, tools or sewing materials at a comfortable height.
  • Adaptive exercise or reaction board: motorised targets and sensors for movement and social competition.

Research what the activity means to the user. Do not assume all older people have the same needs. Commercial components should support your design, not replace the design work.

Writing a focused personal brief

Too broad: Design a motorised shelf for elderly people.
Focused: Design and manufacture a compact working model of an accessible kitchen storage unit for an older person with restricted shoulder movement, lowering frequently used items to a safe working height using large, clearly labelled controls, stopping automatically at its limits.
Measurable specifications: maximum load, vertical travel, operating time, control size, stopping accuracy, successful cycles. The user's situation sets the specification, and the specification sets the tests.

Your next step: the 60-second pitch

Prepare two possible directions. For each one answer: who is the user, what exact task causes difficulty, what part moves, what controls the movement, what will you manufacture yourself, and what three results could prove it works.

Selection check: fits within 500 mm, low-voltage, features remain visible, realistic to finish, enough making skill, clear testing evidence.
