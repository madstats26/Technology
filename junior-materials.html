Materials topic

Woods, metals, plastics, composites, smart materials, properties, joining and finishing.

Materials

Designers choose materials by their properties: how they behave. Always say why a material suits the job.

Material families

FamilyExamplesNotes
WoodsOak, ash (hardwoods); pine (softwood); plywood, MDF (manufactured boards)Hardwoods come from slow-growing broad-leaved trees. Boards are made in large sheets
MetalsSteel, cast iron (ferrous); aluminium, copper, brass (non-ferrous)Ferrous metals contain iron and can rust. Alloys mix metals, such as brass (copper and zinc)
PlasticsAcrylic, polythene, PVC (thermoplastics); epoxy resin, melamine (thermosetting)Thermoplastics can be reheated and reshaped. Thermosetting plastics cannot
CompositesPlywood, GRP, carbon fibreTwo materials combined to give better properties
Smart materialsShape memory alloy, thermochromic filmThey respond to a change in the surroundings, such as heat or light

Choosing a material: ask these questions

  • What properties does the job need?
  • Can I work it with the tools I have?
  • What does it cost, and is it available?
  • Is it safe, and what is its environmental impact?
  • Can it be reused or recycled?

Joining and finishing

  • Wood: screws, nails, glue, dowels. Metal: soldering, riveting, nuts and bolts. Plastic: solvent cement, hot glue, fasteners.
  • Finishes protect and improve appearance: paint, varnish, oil for wood; paint or plastic coating for metal; polishing for plastics.

Sustainability

Use less, reuse, repair and recycle. Choose materials from sustainable sources and design products that last.

Worked examples

1. Why is copper used for electrical wire?

It is a good electrical conductor and is ductile, so it can be drawn into wire and bent easily.

2. Why is acrylic used for a lamp shade?

It is a thermoplastic that can be shaped by heating, it is light and transparent, and it is easy to cut and polish.

3. A designer wants a light, strong, rust-resistant frame. Which material might they choose?

Aluminium: it is low density, strong for its weight and resists corrosion.

4. What is the difference between a thermoplastic and a thermosetting plastic?

A thermoplastic softens when heated and can be reshaped. A thermosetting plastic is set permanently and cannot be reshaped by heating.
