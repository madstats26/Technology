Mechanisms topic

Motion, levers, gears, pulleys, cams and linkages, with worked examples.

Mechanisms

A mechanism changes one type of motion or force into another.

Types of motion

MotionDescriptionExample
RotaryTurning in a circleWheel, drill bit
LinearIn a straight lineA drawer, a sliding door
ReciprocatingBack and forth in a straight lineJigsaw blade, piston
OscillatingSwinging back and forthA pendulum, a swing

Levers

A lever is a rigid bar that turns about a pivot (the fulcrum). Force applied at one point (the effort) moves a load at another.

ClassFulcrum, load, effortExample
FirstFulcrum in the middleSeesaw, scissors
SecondLoad in the middleWheelbarrow, bottle opener
ThirdEffort in the middleTweezers, fishing rod
Moment = force × distance from the fulcrum
Mechanical advantage = load ÷ effort

Gears

  • Meshing gears turn in opposite directions. An idler gear between them makes them turn the same way.
  • Gear ratio = teeth on driven gear ÷ teeth on driver gear
  • A larger driven gear gives a slower output with more turning force. A smaller driven gear gives a faster output with less force.
  • Rack and pinion turns rotary motion into linear. Bevel gears change the direction of drive through 90°. A worm gear gives a big reduction.

Pulleys and belts

  • A belt joins two pulleys. They turn in the same direction (a crossed belt reverses it).
  • The bigger pulley turns slower. Speed ratio = diameter of driven pulley ÷ diameter of driver pulley.
  • A pulley system with several wheels reduces the effort needed to lift a load.

Cams and followers

  • A cam turns rotary motion into reciprocating (up and down) motion.
  • The follower rides on the cam. The cam's shape decides how the follower moves.
  • Circular (eccentric) cam: smooth up and down. Pear cam: a rise, a rest, then a fall. Snail cam: slow rise and a sudden drop.

Linkages

Linkages are bars joined by pivots. They change the direction or size of a motion, for example a windscreen wiper or a folding clothes airer.

Worked examples

1. A 12-tooth driver gear meshes with a 36-tooth driven gear. The driver turns at 600 RPM. Find the speed of the driven gear.

Ratio = 36 ÷ 12 = 3. Output speed = 600 ÷ 3 = 200 RPM.

2. A lever has a 200 N load 100 mm from the fulcrum. The effort is 500 mm from the fulcrum. What effort is needed?

Effort × 500 = 200 × 100, so effort = 20 000 ÷ 500 = 40 N.

3. A load of 600 N is lifted with an effort of 150 N. What is the mechanical advantage?

MA = load ÷ effort = 600 ÷ 150 = 4.

4. A 50 mm driver pulley turns a 150 mm driven pulley. How does the speed change?

Ratio = 150 ÷ 50 = 3, so the driven pulley turns three times slower.

Which cam would you use for a model where a figure rises slowly, then drops suddenly? A snail cam.
