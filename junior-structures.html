Structures and forces

Forces, loads, triangulation, stability, beams and bridges, with worked examples.

Structures and forces

A structure is something built to carry a load, such as a bridge, a shelf, a chair or a crane.

Types of force

ForceWhat it doesExample
TensionPulls and stretchesA cable on a suspension bridge
CompressionPushes and squashesA column holding up a roof
ShearForces slide past each otherA bolt holding two plates
TorsionTwistsA screwdriver, a drive shaft
BendingCombination of tension and compressionA shelf with a book on it

Loads

  • A static load does not move (a book on a shelf).
  • A dynamic load moves or changes (traffic on a bridge, wind on a tower).
  • The self weight of the structure is also a load.

Making structures strong and stable

  • Triangulation: triangles are rigid, so triangles in a frame stop it changing shape.
  • A wide, low base makes a structure more stable. The lower the centre of gravity, the harder it is to tip.
  • Shape: an I-beam, a tube or a folded sheet is stiffer than a flat bar of the same material.
  • Joints: strong joints stop a structure failing at its weakest point.

Beams and bridges

BridgeHow it works
BeamA flat deck supported at each end. The top is in compression and the bottom is in tension
ArchThe curve passes the load down to the supports as compression
TrussA frame of triangles shares the load
SuspensionCables hang from tall towers, so the cables are in tension and the towers in compression

Testing structures

Add the load slowly and watch where it bends or fails. Record the load at failure and improve the weakest point.

Moments

Moment = force × distance from the pivot

A structure balances when the clockwise moments equal the anticlockwise moments.

Worked examples

1. A 200 N force acts 0.5 m from a pivot. What is the moment?

Moment = 200 × 0.5 = 100 N m.

2. A seesaw balances. A 300 N child sits 2 m from the pivot. Where must a 200 N child sit on the other side?

Moments must balance: 200 × d = 300 × 2 = 600, so d = 3 m.

3. A cable fails at 20 kN and the safe working load is 4 kN. What is the factor of safety?

Factor of safety = failure load ÷ safe working load = 20 ÷ 4 = 5.

4. Why is a triangle used in a roof frame?

A triangle cannot change shape unless a side changes length, so it keeps the frame rigid.
