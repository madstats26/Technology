Electronics

Electronics is about controlling small electric currents to make things happen: lights, sounds, movement and decisions.

Input, process, output

  • Input: sends a signal in (switch, LDR, thermistor, microphone).
  • Process: decides what to do (transistor, logic gate, microcontroller).
  • Output: does something (LED, buzzer, motor, lamp).

Ohm's law

V = I × R

Rearranged: I = V ÷ R and R = V ÷ I. Use the units V, A and Ω.

Protecting an LED

R = (supply voltage − LED voltage) ÷ LED current

Worked examples

1. A 9 V battery is connected across a 300 Ω resistor. What current flows?

I = V ÷ R = 9 ÷ 300 = 0.03 A (30 mA).

2. Three resistors of 100 Ω, 220 Ω and 180 Ω are in series. What is the total resistance?

R = 100 + 220 + 180 = 500 Ω.

3. A red LED drops 2 V and needs 20 mA (0.02 A). It runs on a 5 V supply. Find the resistor.

The resistor must drop 5 − 2 = 3 V. R = V ÷ I = 3 ÷ 0.02 = 150 Ω.

4. Why does an LED need a resistor?

An LED has very little resistance on its own. Without a resistor, too much current flows and it can burn out.
